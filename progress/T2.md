## The numbers (these feed T9)

**Execution provider: CPU.** The stock onnxruntime wheel ships no CUDA EP
on linux/aarch64 (`['AzureExecutionProvider', 'CPUExecutionProvider']`),
consistent with the CTranslate2 lesson in CLAUDE.md. Measured anyway and
CPU is comfortably enough: **95 ms per 640×480 embed** (detection +
recognition) ≈ 10 fps capacity against a 2 fps requirement. Decision: stay
on CPU, don't chase a GPU build.

**Score distributions (cosine, buffalo_l, fixture portraits):**

| Pair | Score |
|---|---|
| tracy_a / tracy_b (same sitting) | 0.981 |
| scott_a / scott_b (2014 EMU suit vs 2019) | 0.826 |
| sunita_a / sunita_b (~2004 vs 2018 — hardest pair) | 0.661 |
| all 9 different-person pairs | −0.001 … 0.063 |

**Thresholds:** accept ≥ 0.45, reject < 0.25, ask in between. Both sit
inside a &gt;0.6-wide empty gap; a degraded same-person capture falls into
the ask band ("Maria, is that you?") long before it could be rejected or
misattributed. Enroll-then-identify on the fixture clip: 12/12 frames
matched, min score 0.775.

## Learnings

1. **InsightFace talks on stdout** ("Applied providers…", "find model…"),
   which corrupted the CLI's JSON until the work was wrapped in
   `redirect_stdout(sys.stderr)`. Any future JSON-emitting wrapper around
   these libraries needs the same guard.
2. Per-process model load is ~1.5 s warm — cheap enough that each
   `models` test paying it keeps the tests independent (whole T2 file:
   6 s).
3. The composite multi-face test confirmed largest-face selection is
   real: a quarter-size second face changed the embedding by less than
   the reject threshold.
